## Technical review

The tracker stores elapsed time and a running flag. Start enables movement until delivery completes. Update advances time only while running and ignores non-positive time increments. Progress is elapsed time divided by the 20-second journey duration. Arrival clamps elapsed time to 20 seconds and stops movement. Pause stops movement without clearing progress, so Start resumes the journey. Reset clears elapsed time and the running flag. The dashboard consumes progress to position the parcel and calls the tracking functions from its buttons.

The new sensor module starts at 20 C. Trigger heat raises temperature gradually towards 40 C at 2 C per second, with an alert at 30 C. Stop heat cools towards 20 C. Each impact trigger increments a cumulative count once, and Reset clears both delivery and sensor state. Sensors update independently of parcel movement and remain active while delivery is paused. These are fictional demonstration values, not shipping safety limits.

The app now initialises and updates both modules each frame and displays their readings. I requested a more realistic dashboard, rejected the first light design, and requested a substantially different design. The final preview uses a dark shipment sidebar, a large fictional route schematic, completed-route highlighting, a temperature gauge and impact controls. Arial is loaded from this Mac's system fonts, with Raylib's default font as a fallback. Connection status, timestamped history and CSV export remain unimplemented.
